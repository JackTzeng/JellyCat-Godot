# JellyCat Art Bible

## Core visual identity

JellyCat is a healing, immersive desktop aquarium experience centered on cute jellyfish-cat creatures living and moving inside a tank / bath-like aquatic space.

Primary qualities:

- soothing rather than visually noisy
- cute without becoming babyish or toy-commercial in tone
- soft aquatic depth
- readable silhouettes
- translucent jelly material
- clear cat facial identity
- gentle floating motion
- comfortable for long-duration viewing

## Visual hierarchy

1. JellyCats are the focal subjects.
2. Aquarium space supports mood and depth.
3. Interactive objects remain discoverable but should not overpower the animals.
4. UI must stay legible and visually lighter than the living aquarium.

## Character material language

Prefer:

- semi-translucent gelatinous body
- soft subsurface-like glow or light diffusion
- rounded forms
- subtle internal gradients
- clean highlights rather than hard plastic reflections
- tentacles that feel soft, buoyant, and delayed in motion

Avoid:

- rigid plastic toy surfaces
- aggressive neon glow
- high-frequency texture noise
- horror anatomy
- excessive slime
- photoreal wet-animal fur

## Environment language

Prefer:

- layered water depth
- subtle caustic lighting
- gentle particulate or bubble accents
- calm decorative objects
- enough negative space for multiple JellyCats to move simultaneously

Avoid environments that are so detailed that sprites become hard to read.

## UI language

The UI should feel integrated with the aquarium but remain function-first. Use soft shapes, clear grouping, restrained decoration, and strong state readability.

## Production principle

A beautiful image that cannot be cleanly integrated into Godot is not a finished production asset. Composition, transparency, dimensions, anchors, and state consistency are part of the art standard.
